Numbers

Voice:
하나 / 한
ha-na / han
"One." 하나 stands alone, 한 is the short form used directly in front of a counter word.
둘 / 두
dul / du
"Two." Same standalone-versus-counting split as 하나/한.
셋 / 세
set / se
"Three." The standalone form ends in an unreleased stop, the counting form drops it entirely.
일
il
"One," in the Sino-Korean system. Also means "day" and "work," three unrelated meanings sharing one syllable.
이
i
"Two," Sino-Korean. A single, flat vowel, one of the shortest number words in either system.
삼
sam
"Three," Sino-Korean. Shows up constantly in brand names and building numbers, not just counting.
개
gae
The general counter for objects, paired with native Korean numbers up to 99; from 100 on the number is Sino-Korean, as in 백 개.
명
myeong
The standard counter for people, also paired with native numbers up to 99, then Sino-Korean, as in 백 명.
원
won
The Korean won, always paired with Sino-Korean numbers no matter how large the amount.
시
si
"O'clock." The one major exception to the money-and-dates rule: hours use native numbers, minutes switch to Sino-Korean.
