Reservations and Waiting Lists

Voice:
오늘 저녁 7시에 두 명 예약할 수 있어요?
o-neul jeo-nyeok il-gop-si-e du myeong ye-ya-kal su i-sseo-yo
"Can I book a table for two at 7 tonight?" The order is day, time, number of people, then 예약할 수 있어요?. 7시 is read 일곱 시, with the native number. For other days, swap 오늘 저녁 for 내일 점심 (tomorrow lunch) or 토요일 저녁 (Saturday evening). The staff will then ask your name, 성함이 어떻게 되세요?, and your number, 연락처 알려 주세요. My time words chapter (in Korean Words for Travel) has the days and parts of the day.
예약 안 했는데 자리 있어요?
ye-yak an haen-neun-de ja-ri i-sseo-yo
"I didn't book. Do you have a table?" 자리 is a seat or a table, and -는데 sets up the question gently, like "I didn't book, but...". The staff will ask 몇 분이세요? ("How many people?"); answer with the number and 명: 두 명이요, 세 명이요. If there is no table, the answer is usually 지금은 만석이에요 ("We're full right now") followed by an offer to put you on the waiting list.
얼마나 기다려야 돼요?
eol-ma-na gi-da-ryeo-ya dwae-yo
"How long do I have to wait?" 얼마나 is "how long" or "how much", and -야 돼요 is "have to". The staff may answer in minutes, 삼십 분 정도요 ("about thirty minutes"), or in groups ahead of you, 앞에 다섯 팀 있어요 ("There are five groups ahead"). 팀 is the word for a party of diners. Minutes take Sino-Korean numbers, and groups take native ones.
대기 걸어 주세요
dae-gi geo-reo ju-se-yo
"Please put me on the waiting list." 대기 is waiting and 걸다 is "to put down" or "to register", the same verb as hanging something up. Many places say 웨이팅 instead: 웨이팅 걸게요 is "I'll put my name down". If there is a tablet at the door, the staff will point to it with 여기 번호 입력해 주세요 ("Enter your number here"). The tablet usually wants a Korean mobile number for a text when your turn comes.
순서 되면 연락 주세요
sun-seo doe-myeon yeol-lak ju-se-yo
"Please contact me when it's my turn." 순서 is "turn" or "order", and 연락 is contact by call or text. Say it after card 4 if you want to walk around nearby. Without a Korean number, a call or text may not reach you, so ask the alternative: 이름 불러 주실 수 있어요? ("Could you call out my name?"), and stay within earshot. My phone and Wi-Fi words chapter (in Korean Words for Travel) covers getting a local number.
예약 시간을 바꿀 수 있을까요?
ye-yak si-ga-neul ba-kkul su i-sseul-kka-yo
"Could I change my booking time?" 바꾸다 is "to change", and -을까요 makes it a soft request. Follow it with the new time: 여덟 시로 바꿀 수 있을까요? ("Could I change it to eight?"). To change the number of people instead, say 인원을 세 명으로 바꿀 수 있을까요? ("Could I make it three people?"). Call or message as early as you can; small restaurants plan their ingredients around bookings.
예약 취소하고 싶어요
ye-yak chwi-so-ha-go si-peo-yo
"I'd like to cancel my booking." Start with 죄송한데요 ("I'm sorry, but...") and give your name and booking time so the staff can find it. Cancelling early matters: a restaurant that warned you in advance, for example by text, may charge a no-show penalty (the limits are in the questions at the end). Read the booking message for any 예약금 (deposit) or 위약금 (penalty) rules.
