Chicken and Beer

후라이드 반 양념 반으로 주세요
hu-ra-i-deu ban yang-nyeom ba-neu-ro ju-se-yo
"Half fried, half seasoned, please." 후라이드 is plain fried chicken, and 양념 (seasoning) is the sticky sweet and spicy red sauce. 반 is "half". Most places sell a half-and-half platter, which menus call 반반, so 반반으로 주세요 says the same thing in two words. Other common flavours are 간장 (soy sauce and garlic) and 파닭 (chicken under a pile of sliced green onion). The standard spelling of the loanword is 프라이드, but menus almost always write 후라이드.
순살로 돼요?
sun-sal-lo dwae-yo
"Can I get it boneless?" 순살 is boneless meat, and 뼈 (bone) is the bone-in version, the default at many places. Some shops charge more for boneless, so look at the menu or ask 순살은 추가 금액 있어요? ("Is there an extra charge for boneless?"). Boneless chicken is easier to share and to eat with chopsticks, which is why groups often pick it.
생맥주 오백 두 잔 주세요
saeng-maek-ju o-baek du jan ju-se-yo
"Two 500 cc draft beers, please." 생맥주 is draft beer, and pubs list glass sizes in cc on the menu, so 오백 (five hundred) is the usual glass and the number is the size, not the price. For a pitcher, look for 피처 on the menu. If you want a bottle of soju with it, say 소주 한 병 주세요; 병 is the counter for bottles. Water is usually free: 물 좀 주세요.
치킨무 좀 더 주세요
chi-kin-mu jom deo ju-se-yo
"A bit more pickled radish, please." 치킨무 is the sweet and sour radish cubes served with chicken. It and other small side dishes are normally refilled when you ask. 좀 softens the request and 더 means "more". The same pattern works for anything on the table: 소스 좀 더 주세요 (more sauce), 앞접시 좀 주세요 (some small plates), 물티슈 좀 주세요 (wet wipes).
덜 매운 메뉴 있어요?
deol mae-un me-nyu i-sseo-yo
"Do you have anything less spicy?" 덜 is "less" and 맵다 is "spicy hot". Chicken sauces are made in advance, so asking the kitchen to change the heat rarely works. Asking for a milder menu item does. Expect the staff to point to 후라이드 or 간장. If you are the one who likes heat, ask 제일 매운 게 뭐예요? ("What's the spiciest?"). My taste words chapter (in Korean Words for Travel) has more words for spicy, salty and sweet.
남은 거 포장해 주세요
na-meun geo po-jang-hae ju-se-yo
"Please pack up what's left." 남다 is "to be left over" and 포장 is "packing" or "takeaway". Chicken portions are large, and taking the rest home is normal. Say it when you ask for the bill, or call a staff member over with 저기요 ("Excuse me"). If you want to order to take away from the start, say 포장이요.
계산은 따로 할게요
gye-sa-neun tta-ro hal-ge-yo
"We'll pay separately." 계산 is the bill and 따로 is "separately". Many pubs ask one person to pay at the counter, and splitting by item takes the staff time, so a common alternative is one card for the table and a bank transfer between friends. If you want to pay together, say 같이 계산할게요. To ask for the bill at the table, say 계산할게요 or walk to the counter.
