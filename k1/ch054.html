Book 1: Korean Phrases for Travel · Chapter 54

Seeing a Doctor

접수하려고요
jeop-su-ha-ryeo-go-yo
"I'd like to register" (literally "I'm intending to do registration"). Say it at the front desk. Useful add-ons: 처음이에요 (cheo-eum-i-e-yo, "it's my first time here"), 예약 안 했어요 (ye-yak an hae-sseo-yo, "I didn't make a reservation") and 여권 여기 있어요 (yeo-gwon yeo-gi i-sseo-yo, "here is my passport") if they ask for ID.
목이 아프고 열이 나요
mo-gi a-peu-go yeo-ri na-yo
"My throat hurts and I have a fever" (literally "a fever comes out"). The doctor will probably open with 어디가 불편하세요? (eo-di-ga bul-pyeon-ha-se-yo, "where does it bother you?") or 어디가 아프세요? (eo-di-ga a-peu-se-yo, "where does it hurt?"). Swap in other symptoms: 기침이 나요 (gi-chi-mi na-yo, "I have a cough"), 콧물이 나요 (kon-mu-ri na-yo, "I have a runny nose"), 어지러워요 (eo-ji-reo-wo-yo, "I feel dizzy"), 토했어요 (to-hae-sseo-yo, "I threw up").
이틀 전부터 아파요
i-teul jeon-bu-teo a-pa-yo
"It has hurt since two days ago." The doctor will ask 언제부터 아팠어요? (eon-je-bu-teo a-pa-sseo-yo, "since when has it hurt?"). Answer with a time word + 전부터: 어제부터요 (eo-je-bu-teo-yo, "since yesterday"), 오늘 아침부터요 (o-neul a-chim-bu-teo-yo, "since this morning"), 일주일 전부터요 (il-ju-il jeon-bu-teo-yo, "since a week ago"). Add 점점 심해져요 (jeom-jeom sim-hae-jyeo-yo, "it's getting worse") if it is. For other day counts, see the number words (in Korean Words for Travel).
약 알레르기는 없어요
yak al-le-reu-gi-neun eop-seo-yo
"I don't have any drug allergies." The question you will hear is 알레르기 있으세요? (al-le-reu-gi i-sseu-se-yo, "do you have any allergies?"). If you do, say the item and switch the ending: 음식 알레르기가 있어요 (eum-sik al-le-reu-gi-ga i-sseo-yo, "I have a food allergy"). For a specific medicine such as penicillin, the sentence is in the pharmacy chapter.
지금 먹는 약이 있어요
ji-geum meong-neun ya-gi i-sseo-yo
"I'm taking some medicine right now" (literally "there is medicine I'm taking now"). The doctor may ask 드시는 약 있으세요? (deu-si-neun yak i-sseu-se-yo, "are you taking any medicine?"). Show a photo of the box or label and say 이 약이에요 (i ya-gi-e-yo, "it's this medicine"). If you take none, say 먹는 약 없어요 (meong-neun yak eop-seo-yo). If it applies, 임신 중이에요 (im-sin jung-i-e-yo) means "I'm pregnant".
수납은 어디서 해요?
su-na-beun eo-di-seo hae-yo
"Where do I pay?" 수납 is the word on hospital payment windows, and the dictionary defines it as receiving money. Follow-ups: 카드로 할게요 (ka-deu-ro hal-ge-yo, "I'll pay by card"), 진료비가 얼마예요? (jil-lyo-bi-ga eol-ma-ye-yo, "how much is the medical fee?") and 영수증 주세요 (yeong-su-jeung ju-se-yo, "receipt, please"). Keep the receipt if your insurer will ask for it. More payment words are in the shopping words (in Korean Words for Travel).
처방전은 어디서 받아요?
cheo-bang-jeo-neun eo-di-seo ba-da-yo
"Where do I get the prescription?" 처방전 is a document listing the medicine or treatment needed, according to the dictionary. After that, you need a pharmacy: 가까운 약국이 어디예요? (ga-kka-un yak-gu-gi eo-di-ye-yo, "where is the nearest pharmacy?"). Hand the slip over there, and the pharmacy phrases pick up from that point.
