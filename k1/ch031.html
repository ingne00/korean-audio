Book 1: Korean Phrases for Travel · Chapter 31

The Bakery

쟁반이랑 집게는 어디 있어요?
jaeng-ba-ni-rang jip-ge-neun eo-di i-sseo-yo
"Where are the tray and tongs?" 이랑 means "and" in everyday speech, and 하고 works the same way.
갓 구운 빵 있어요?
gat gu-un ppang i-sseo-yo
"Do you have any freshly baked bread?" If you do not know the name of the bread, say 갓 구운 거 있어요? (gat gu-un geo i-sseo-yo, "Do you have anything just baked?").
데워 드릴까요?
de-wo deu-ril-kka-yo
"Shall I warm it up for you?" This is the line you hear, not the one you say. Two ways to answer: 네, 데워 주세요 (ne, de-wo ju-se-yo, "Yes, please warm it up") or 아니요, 그냥 주세요 (a-ni-yo, geu-nyang ju-se-yo, "No, just give it to me as it is").
포장해 주세요.
po-jang-hae ju-se-yo
"To go, please." If you are eating at the shop, say 여기서 먹고 갈게요 (yeo-gi-seo meok-go gal-ge-yo, "I'll eat here"). A clerk who wants to know which one you mean can ask 드시고 가세요? (deu-si-go ga-se-yo, "Will you eat here?").
이건 따로 포장해 주세요.
i-geon tta-ro po-jang-hae ju-se-yo
"Please wrap this one separately." 이건 is the spoken short form of 이것은 ("this one"). For several items, 따로따로 means "each one apart": 따로따로 포장해 주세요 (tta-ro-tta-ro po-jang-hae ju-se-yo, "Please wrap them each separately").
식빵은 슬라이스해 주세요.
sik-ppang-eun seul-la-i-seu-hae ju-se-yo
"Please slice the loaf." A plain-verb version is 식빵은 썰어 주세요 (sik-ppang-eun sseo-reo ju-se-yo, "Please cut the loaf"). Shops differ, so this is a request, not something you can count on.
박스에 담아 주세요.
bak-seu-e da-ma ju-se-yo
"Please put it in a box." 상자에 담아 주세요 (sang-ja-e da-ma ju-se-yo) means the same thing. For counting, 한 박스 (han bak-seu) is "one box".
