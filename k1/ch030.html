Allergies and Vegetarian Food

Voice:
저는 채식주의자예요
jeo-neun chae-sik-ju-ui-ja-ye-yo
"I'm a vegetarian." 채식 is a plant-based diet and 채식주의자 is a person who keeps one. Koreans use the word loosely: to some people it allows fish or eggs. That is why card 2 matters more than this one. If you are vegan, say 저는 비건이에요 (jeo-neun bi-geo-ni-e-yo); 비건 is now common on menus and packaging.
고기랑 해산물은 못 먹어요
go-gi-rang hae-san-mu-reun mot meo-geo-yo
"I can't eat meat or seafood." 고기 is meat, 해산물 is seafood, and 랑 is "and". VisitKorea suggests preparing exactly this kind of line, such as "I cannot eat meat, eggs, and seafood" (VisitKorea). To add eggs, say 고기, 해산물, 계란은 못 먹어요. To add dairy, add 우유. Using 못 (cannot) instead of 안 (do not) tells the staff it is a limit, not a preference.
땅콩 알레르기가 있어요
ttang-kong al-le-reu-gi-ga i-sseo-yo
"I have a peanut allergy." Put your allergen in front of 알레르기: 우유 (milk), 계란 or 달걀 (egg), 밀 (wheat), 대두 or 콩 (soy), 새우 (shrimp), 게 (crab), 조개 (shellfish), 견과류 (tree nuts), 메밀 (buckwheat), 복숭아 (peach). For a severe allergy, write the sentence on a card or phone screen too, so the staff can show it to the kitchen.
이거 고기 들어가요?
i-geo go-gi deu-reo-ga-yo
"Does this have meat in it?" 들어가다 is "to go in", and in a kitchen it means "to be an ingredient". Point at the menu item as you ask. Swap 고기 for anything: 계란 들어가요? (egg), 새우 들어가요? (shrimp), 젓갈 들어가요? (salted seafood). VisitKorea warns that even vegetable gimbap may contain ham, egg or crab stick depending on the restaurant, and that bibimbap toppings often include meat and egg (VisitKorea), so ask even when the name sounds safe.
육수는 뭐로 만들어요?
yuk-su-neun mwo-ro man-deu-reo-yo
"What's the broth made with?" 육수 is soup stock. Common answers are 멸치 (anchovy), 다시마 (kelp), 사골 (beef bone), 닭 (chicken) and 해물 (seafood). Kelp alone is plant-based; the others are not. Ask this for noodle soups, stews and anything with 국 or 탕 in its name. A yes-or-no version is 육수에 고기나 생선 들어가요? ("Is there meat or fish in the broth?").
고기 빼고 해 주실 수 있어요?
go-gi ppae-go hae ju-sil su i-sseo-yo
"Could you make it without meat?" 빼고 is "leaving out". This works for dishes where the meat is a topping, such as bibimbap or cold noodles, and not for dishes cooked in a meat broth. Swap in the ingredient: 계란 빼고 (without egg), 젓갈 빼고 (without salted seafood). If the answer is 안 돼요, the dish is made in advance, so pick something else.
조금이라도 들어가면 안 돼요
jo-geu-mi-ra-do deu-reo-ga-myeon an dwae-yo
"Even a little is not OK." 조금이라도 is "even a small amount". Say it after card 3 if your allergy is serious. It tells the cook that picking the peanuts off the top is not enough, and it covers shared oil, sauces and garnishes. Korean staff take this sentence seriously; it is the difference between a dislike and a medical problem.
