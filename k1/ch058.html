Compliments and Polite Refusals

Voice:
정말 예뻐요
jeong-mal ye-ppeo-yo
"It's really pretty" or "You look really pretty." 예쁘다 works for people, clothes, places, cafes and even a well-packed lunch box. For a man, or for anything that is cool rather than pretty, use 멋있어요 (meo-si-sseo-yo), "That's cool / You look great." 잘생겼어요 (jal-saeng-gyeo-sseo-yo) means "handsome". In casual speech, 정말 often becomes 진짜 (jin-jja), "really": 진짜 예뻐요 sounds a little younger and more excited, and you will hear it far more often among friends than in a shop or an office.
잘 어울려요
jal eo-ul-lyeo-yo
"It suits you." This is the go-to line for clothes, a new haircut, glasses or a hanbok. Add the item at the front: 그 색 잘 어울려요 (geu saek jal eo-ul-lyeo-yo), "That color suits you." Shop staff say this to you constantly, so it is worth recognizing too; the clothing store words (in Korean Words for Travel) cover sizes and fitting.
정말 맛있어요
jeong-mal ma-si-sseo-yo
"This is really delicious." Say it while you are eating, not only at the end. After the meal, the set phrase is 잘 먹었습니다 (jal meo-geot-seum-ni-da), "I ate well, thank you," said to whoever cooked or paid. To praise one dish, point to it and say 이거 정말 맛있어요. The taste words (in Korean Words for Travel) let you say why.
아니에요, 아직 멀었어요
a-ni-e-yo, a-jik meo-reo-sseo-yo
"Oh no, I still have a long way to go." This is the classic answer when someone praises your Korean, your cooking or your work. 멀었어요 literally means "it is still far". 감사합니다 is also fine and is heard more often among younger people, but the modest answer sounds warm and natural at any age.
괜찮아요, 감사합니다
gwaen-cha-na-yo, gam-sa-ham-ni-da
"No, thank you. I'm fine." 괜찮아요 means "it's fine", and with a small wave of the hand it becomes the everyday way to turn down an offer: more food, a shopping bag, a seat, a flyer. Adding 감사합니다 makes it clear that you are declining, not accepting, since 괜찮아요 on its own can also mean "yes, that's fine". At a shop counter, when the clerk asks 봉투 필요하세요? (bong-tu pi-ryo-ha-se-yo), "Do you need a bag?", a quick 괜찮아요 is all you need. With a street flyer or a free sample, a small nod and the same word work just as well.
배불러서 더는 못 먹겠어요
bae-bul-leo-seo deo-neun mot meok-ge-sseo-yo
"I'm so full I can't eat any more." Giving a reason is the polite part: 배불러서 means "because I'm full". Korean hosts often offer food more than once, so saying this with a smile, and with a compliment from card 3, lets them stop offering without feeling refused.
마음만 받을게요
ma-eum-man ba-deul-ge-yo
"I'll accept just the kind thought." This turns down a gift, a treat or a favor while thanking the person for the offer. It is gentle but clear, and it works well when someone insists on paying or on giving you something you cannot carry. Follow it with 감사합니다.
