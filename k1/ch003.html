When You Don't Understand

죄송합니다
joe-song-ham-ni-da
"I'm sorry." This is the formal apology, and the right one to open with when you have not understood a stranger. It also works as "excuse me" when you squeeze past someone. The softer polite form 죄송해요 (joe-song-hae-yo) is fine with staff. Both are more polite than 미안해요, which is covered in the FAQ.
잘 못 알아들었어요
jal mot a-ra-deu-reo-sseo-yo
"I didn't catch that." 알아듣다 means to understand something you hear, so this line says the problem is your ear, not the other person's words. It is the most natural answer to fast speech. Note the space: 잘 못 ("not well") is two words here. Written together, 잘못 means "a mistake" or "wrongly".
다시 한번 말씀해 주시겠어요?
da-si han-beon mal-sseum-hae ju-si-ge-sseo-yo
"Could you say that once more?" 말씀 is the polite word for someone's speech, and -시겠어요? makes it a gentle request. In a hurry, the short form 다시요? (da-si-yo) with a rising tone does the same job. 한번 is written as one word in 다시 한번.
천천히 말씀해 주세요
cheon-cheon-hi mal-sseum-hae ju-se-yo
"Please speak slowly." Use it when you understand some words and need more time for the rest. Most people slow down right away. If they repeat at the same speed, add 좀 ("a bit") and a smile: 좀 천천히요.
한국어를 잘 못해요
han-gu-geo-reul jal mo-tae-yo
"I don't speak Korean well." It tells the other person to simplify, and it often makes them switch to gestures, numbers on a calculator or English. Here 못하다 ("to be poor at") is written as one word. A gentler version is 한국어를 조금 해요, "I speak a little Korean".
영어 하실 줄 아세요?
yeong-eo ha-sil jul a-se-yo
"Do you speak English?" The -(으)ㄹ 줄 알다 pattern asks whether someone knows how to do something, and -시- keeps it respectful. Ask it after a greeting, not as the first thing you say. A shorter form you will hear is 영어 되세요? (yeong-eo doe-se-yo), "does English work for you?"
여기에 좀 써 주시겠어요?
yeo-gi-e jom sseo ju-si-ge-sseo-yo
"Could you write it here?" Hold out your phone's notes app or a piece of paper. Numbers, times and addresses are much easier to read than to hear, and you can paste written Korean into a translation app. For numbers, see the number words chapter (in Korean Words for Travel).
