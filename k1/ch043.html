Book 1: Korean Phrases for Travel · Chapter 43

The Jjimjilbang

Voice:
한 명이에요. 열쇠하고 찜질복 주세요
han myeong-i-e-yo. yeol-soe-ha-go jjim-jil-bok ju-se-yo
"It's one person. A locker key and a jjimjilbang outfit, please." 한 명 is "one person", and 하고 means "and". Whether the counter hands you a key, a numbered band or both depends on the place, so take whatever they give you.
탈의실은 어디예요?
ta-rui-si-reun eo-di-ye-yo
"Where is the changing room?" Swap in 목욕탕은 (mo-gyok-tang-eun, "the bath"), 사우나는 (sa-u-na-neun, "the sauna") or 찜질방은 (jjim-jil-bang-eun, "the jjimjilbang floor").
수건은 어디 있어요?
su-geo-neun eo-di i-sseo-yo
"Where are the towels?" To ask for more, say 수건 하나 더 주세요 (su-geon ha-na deo ju-se-yo, "one more towel, please").
세신은 얼마예요?
se-si-neun eol-ma-ye-yo
"How much is a body scrub (seshin)?" Point at the price list if there is one: the Korea.net dialogue shows a posted list.
몇 시까지 있을 수 있어요?
myeot si-kka-ji i-sseul su i-sseo-yo
"Until what time can I stay?" A reply such as 열 시까지요 ("until ten o'clock") uses a native number for the hour, as in my number words chapter (in Korean Words for Travel). If you would rather ask about the length of the stay, say 몇 시간 있을 수 있어요? (myeot si-gan i-sseul su i-sseo-yo, "how many hours can I stay?").
수면실은 어디예요?
su-myeon-si-reun eo-di-ye-yo
"Where is the sleeping room?" 수면실 is the room for a short sleep. To ask about a place to sleep without naming the room, say 잘 수 있는 곳이 어디예요? (jal su in-neun go-si eo-di-ye-yo, "where is a place I can sleep?").
락커는 어디에 있나요?
rak-keo-neun eo-di-e in-na-yo
"Where is the locker?" My suggestion: have your band or key with its number ready if the staff ask which locker. If you lost the key, say 열쇠를 잃어버렸어요 (yeol-soe-reul i-reo-beo-ryeo-sseo-yo, "I lost the key").
