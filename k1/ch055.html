Reporting a Theft to the Police

경찰서가 어디예요?
gyeong-chal-seo-ga eo-di-ye-yo
"Where is the police station?" Ask a shop clerk or a station attendant. If they point you to a 지구대 or 파출소 instead, go there: these smaller offices take reports too, and they are usually closer. To ask for any nearby police office, swap in 근처에 파출소 있어요? (geun-cheo-e pa-chul-so i-sseo-yo), "Is there a police box nearby?" For more ways to ask the way, see the directions phrases.
도난 신고하러 왔어요
do-nan sin-go-ha-reo wa-sseo-yo
"I came to report a theft." 도난 is theft, 신고 is a report to the authorities, and -러 왔어요 means "I came in order to". This is the sentence to say as you walk in, so the officer knows what kind of form to pull up. For a loss with no theft, say 분실 신고하러 왔어요 (bun-sil sin-go-ha-reo wa-sseo-yo), "I came to report something lost."
지갑을 도둑맞았어요
ji-ga-beul do-dung-ma-ja-sseo-yo
"My wallet was stolen." 도둑 is a thief, and 도둑맞다 is the everyday verb for "to be robbed of" something. Replace 지갑 with the item: 휴대폰 (hyu-dae-pon, phone), 가방 (ga-bang, bag), 카드 (ka-deu, card). It also helps to write a short list of what was inside: the card names, roughly how much cash, and the color and brand of the item.
여권을 잃어버렸어요
yeo-gwo-neul i-reo-beo-ryeo-sseo-yo
"I lost my passport." If you are sure it was taken, say 여권을 도둑맞았어요 instead, since the report type differs. The police report is the first step; the replacement comes from your own embassy, and embassies set their own rules. The Royal Thai Embassy in Seoul, for one, lists a "police report (in case of a lost passport)" for its emergency travel document, while the Singapore Embassy in Seoul handles the loss report and its temporary document online. Check your embassy's page before you go.
지하철에서 없어진 것 같아요
ji-ha-cheo-re-seo eop-seo-jin geot ga-ta-yo
"I think it went missing on the subway." 없어지다 is "to disappear", and 것 같아요 softens the line to "I think". Swap the place: 택시에서 (taek-si-e-seo, in a taxi), 버스에서 (beo-seu-e-seo, on a bus), 카페에서 (ka-pe-e-seo, at a cafe). Give the line, the direction and the time if you know them; the subway words (in Korean Words for Travel) help with that.
영어 통역 가능할까요?
yeong-eo tong-yeok ga-neung-hal-kka-yo
"Could I get English interpretation?" 통역 is interpretation. English and Chinese interpreters work from a center run by the Korean National Police Agency, and in March 2024 the center switched from weekday hours to round-the-clock operation (Korea.net, March 2024). Its interpreters were trained in reporting procedure and legal terms, and they also support officers on site (Korea.net, July 2023). Say this line early and then switch to English if they connect you.
신고 확인서 받을 수 있을까요?
sin-go hwa-gin-seo ba-deul su i-sseul-kka-yo
"Could I get a document confirming my report?" 확인서 is a written confirmation. Ask before you leave, and ask which paper fits your need: an embassy, an insurer and a card company may each want something different. If the officer gives you a case number, take a photo of it. Write down the name of the office as well, so you can find the paper again.
