Ordering at a Cafe

Voice:
아이스 아메리카노 한 잔 주세요
a-i-seu a-me-ri-ka-no han jan ju-se-yo
"One iced americano, please." The pattern is drink + number + 잔 (the counter for cups and glasses) + 주세요. For two, say 두 잔; for three, 세 잔. 아이스 goes in front of the drink, so an iced latte is 아이스 라떼. Koreans shorten the iced americano to 아아 (a-a), and you will hear it in line, but the full name is clearer when you order.
따뜻한 걸로 주세요
tta-tteu-tan geol-lo ju-se-yo
"A hot one, please." 따뜻하다 is "warm", and on cafe menus it is the word for a hot drink. 걸로 is short for 것으로, "the one that is". Use it when the staff ask 아이스로 드릴까요, 따뜻한 걸로 드릴까요? ("Iced or hot?"), or put it in front of the drink: 따뜻한 라떼 한 잔 주세요. You will also hear 핫 (hot) on menus and in quick speech.
여기서 먹고 갈게요
yeo-gi-seo meok-go gal-ge-yo
"I'll have it here." Word for word it is "I'll eat here and go", and Koreans use 먹다 for drinks in this phrase too. The staff usually ask first: 드시고 가세요? ("Having it here?") or 가져가세요? ("Taking it away?"). For takeaway, answer 가져갈게요 (ga-jyeo-gal-ge-yo) or 테이크아웃이요. The answer matters because the cup changes: under a voluntary agreement signed on July 14, 2026 and in effect from September 2026, participating chains such as Starbucks, Ediya Coffee and Paris Baguette serve dine-in drinks in reusable cups (World Coffee Portal).
샷 추가해 주세요
syat chu-ga-hae ju-se-yo
"Add a shot, please." 추가 is "addition", and it works for anything you want more of: 시럽 추가 (extra syrup), 휘핑 추가 (extra whipped cream). Say it right after the drink name. Extras usually cost a little more, and the price is on the menu board or the kiosk screen. For the opposite, a weaker coffee, say 연하게 해 주세요 (yeon-ha-ge hae ju-se-yo), "Make it mild, please."
시럽은 빼 주세요
si-reo-beun ppae ju-se-yo
"No syrup, please." 빼다 is "to take out", and 빼 주세요 is the line for leaving anything out: 얼음 빼 주세요 is no ice, 휘핑 빼 주세요 is no whipped cream. Some sweet drinks are made from a premixed base, so the staff may answer 이건 빼기 어려워요 ("That one is hard to leave out"). In that case, 덜 달게 해 주세요 ("Make it less sweet") is a softer request. For more taste words, see my taste words chapter (in Korean Words for Travel).
오트 우유로 바꿀 수 있어요?
o-teu u-yu-ro ba-kkul su i-sseo-yo
"Can I change it to oat milk?" 바꾸다 is "to change" and -로 marks what you change it to. Many cafes write it 오트밀크 on the menu, and some offer 두유 (soy milk) instead. For a lactose problem, say 우유를 못 먹어요 ("I can't have milk") so the staff know it is not just a taste choice. If the shop does not have it, the reply is 없어요 or 안 돼요.
영수증은 버려 주세요
yeong-su-jeung-eun beo-ryeo ju-se-yo
"Please throw the receipt away." After you pay, the staff often ask 영수증 드릴까요? ("Would you like the receipt?"). This is the polite way to say no. If you want it, say 네, 주세요. When you pay by card, the staff may also hand the card back with 진동벨 드릴게요, "Here's your pager": it buzzes when your drink is ready at the pickup counter.
