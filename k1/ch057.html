Book 1: Korean Phrases for Travel · Chapter 57

Small Talk

어디에서 오셨어요?
eo-di-e-seo o-syeo-sseo-yo
"Where are you from?" This is how you will most often hear the question, and how to ask it back. 오셨어요 is the honorific past of 오다, "to come", so the line literally asks where the person came from. The shorter 어디서 오셨어요? (eo-di-seo o-syeo-sseo-yo) is just as common. Among friends of the same age, the informal version is 어디서 왔어? (eo-di-seo wa-sseo), but keep that for people who already speak to you in 반말 (casual speech).
저는 캐나다에서 왔어요
jeo-neun kae-na-da-e-seo wa-sseo-yo
"I'm from Canada." Put your country in place of 캐나다: 미국 (mi-guk, the United States), 영국 (yeong-guk, the United Kingdom), 호주 (ho-ju, Australia), 독일 (do-gil, Germany), 프랑스 (peu-rang-seu, France). Note that the answer uses plain 왔어요, not 오셨어요, because you do not use the honorific about yourself.
한국에 여행 왔어요
han-gu-ge yeo-haeng wa-sseo-yo
"I came to Korea on a trip." The usual follow-up to "where are you from" is why you are here, often asked as 여행 오셨어요? (yeo-haeng o-syeo-sseo-yo), "Are you here on a trip?" Swap 여행 for 출장 (chul-jang, a business trip), 공부하러 (gong-bu-ha-reo, to study) or 친구 만나러 (chin-gu man-na-reo, to see a friend). If you live in Korea, say 한국에 살아요 (han-gu-ge sa-ra-yo), "I live in Korea," and the next question will probably be how long you have been here, which card 4 answers.
한국에 온 지 일주일 됐어요
han-gu-ge on ji il-ju-il dwae-sseo-yo
"I've been in Korea for a week." The pattern is "verb + ㄴ 지 + time + 됐어요", "it has been (time) since". Swap 일주일 for 사흘 (sa-heul, three days), 열흘 (yeol-heul, ten days) or 한 달 (han dal, a month). 지 is written apart from 온, a spacing point many learners miss.
한국어 조금 할 줄 알아요
han-gu-geo jo-geum hal jul a-ra-yo
"I can speak a little Korean." This line usually earns a compliment, so be ready with a modest reply: 아직 잘 못해요 (a-jik jal mo-tae-yo), "I'm not good yet." -ㄹ 줄 알다 means "to know how to", and 줄 is written apart from 할.
무슨 일 하세요?
mu-seun il ha-se-yo
"What do you do for work?" This is a normal question in Korea, not a nosy one, and you will be asked it too. Answer with 저는 (job)이에요 / 예요, such as 저는 교사예요 (jeo-neun gyo-sa-ye-yo), "I'm a teacher", or 회사 다녀요 (hoe-sa da-nyeo-yo), "I work at a company." To move on to hobbies, the hobby words (in Korean Words for Travel) give you more to say.
날씨가 많이 추워졌네요
nal-ssi-ga ma-ni chu-wo-jyeon-ne-yo
"It's got a lot colder, hasn't it?" Weather is the easiest way to start or restart a conversation. -네요 marks something you have just noticed and invites the other person to agree. For hot days, swap in 더워졌네요 (deo-wo-jyeon-ne-yo), "It's got hotter." The weather words (in Korean Words for Travel) give you rain, dust and seasons to work with.
