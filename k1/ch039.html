The Tailor Shop

바지 기장 좀 줄여 주세요
ba-ji gi-jang jom ju-ryeo ju-se-yo
"Could you shorten these trousers?" 바지 is trousers, 기장 is the length of a garment, 줄여 comes from 줄이다 (to make smaller), and 주세요 is "please do it for me".
허리 좀 줄여 주세요
heo-ri jom ju-ryeo ju-se-yo
"Could you take in the waist?" Say it while you point at the waistband of the trousers or skirt.
지퍼가 안 올라가요. 고쳐 주세요.
ji-peo-ga an ol-la-ga-yo. go-chyeo ju-se-yo
"The zipper won't go up. Please fix it." 지퍼 is the zipper, and 고쳐 comes from 고치다, to fix.
단추가 떨어졌어요. 달아 주세요.
dan-chu-ga tteo-reo-jyeo-sseo-yo. da-ra ju-se-yo
"A button came off. Please put it back on." 단추 is a button, and 달아 comes from 달다, to attach. My suggestion: if one button is missing from a row, show the shop the others, and use the native numbers from my number words chapter (in Korean Words for Travel) to say how many: 하나, 두 개, 세 개.
수선비 얼마예요?
su-seon-bi eol-ma-ye-yo
"How much is the repair fee?" 수선비 is the fee for 수선 (repair or alteration), and 얼마예요 is "how much is it". Ask the price before you leave the clothes, because alteration prices vary by shop. See also my shopping words (in Korean Words for Travel).
언제 찾으러 오면 돼요?
eon-je cha-jeu-reo o-myeon dwae-yo
"When should I come to pick it up?" 오면 돼요 literally means "is it fine if I come", so the question asks when to come. Turnaround time for alterations varies, so ask each shop.
맡긴 옷 찾으러 왔어요
mat-gin ot cha-jeu-reo wa-sseo-yo
"I'm here to pick up the clothes I left." My suggestion: give your name or show the slip if you have one.
