Hello, Thank You and Sorry

안녕하세요
an-nyeong-ha-se-yo
"Hello." This one line works at any hour, so there is no separate good morning or good evening to learn for daily use. Say it when you walk into a shop, a clinic or a hotel lobby, and when a staff member greets you first. A small nod of the head goes with it; the bowing etiquette chapter (in Korean Culture Explained) explains how deep to go for different people.
감사합니다
gam-sa-ham-ni-da
"Thank you." This is the form you hear from shop staff, bus drivers and announcements, and it is never too formal for a visitor to use. Say it when someone hands you change, holds a door or points you the right way. The softer 고맙습니다 (go-map-seum-ni-da) means the same and is just as polite; you will hear both all day.
안녕히 계세요
an-nyeong-hi gye-se-yo
"Goodbye" when you are the one leaving and the other person stays. Word for word it means "stay in peace". This is the line for walking out of a shop, a restaurant or a hotel front desk, because the staff are staying behind.
안녕히 가세요
an-nyeong-hi ga-se-yo
"Goodbye" when the other person is leaving. Word for word it means "go in peace". A shop owner says this to you as you leave. You will use it most when a host or a new friend leaves a place where you are staying, such as your guesthouse lobby, or when two people part on the street and both walk off. In that last case, both people say 안녕히 가세요.
저기요
jeo-gi-yo
"Excuse me" to get someone's attention, most often a server in a restaurant. It literally points "over there", and with a raised hand it means "could you come here?". Say it at a normal volume; in a busy restaurant it is expected, not rude. For a stranger on the street, 저기요 or 실례합니다 (sil-lye-ham-ni-da, "excuse me") both work before a question.
잘 먹었습니다
jal meo-geot-seum-ni-da
"I ate well", said after a meal as thanks to whoever cooked, paid or served. Say it to the owner on the way out of a small restaurant, or to a host who fed you. Before the meal, the matching line is 잘 먹겠습니다 (jal meok-get-seum-ni-da), "I will eat well". Ordering and paying lines are in the restaurant phrases chapter.
괜찮아요
gwaen-cha-na-yo
"It's okay" or "no, thank you". This is the answer to an apology (someone bumps you on the subway and says 죄송합니다), and also the polite way to turn down an offer such as a plastic bag, a receipt or a free sample. Add a small shake of the hand, palm out, to make the "no thanks" meaning clear. With rising intonation, 괜찮아요? asks "Are you all right?"
