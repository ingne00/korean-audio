Polite Speech in One Chapter

Voice:
여기 앉아도 돼요?
yeo-gi an-ja-do dwae-yo
"May I sit here?" This is plain polite speech: an ordinary verb with -요 on the end. Take the -요 off and you have 앉아도 돼?, which is what you would say to a close friend. That single syllable is the difference between polite and casual in most of what you say on a trip, so when in doubt, end with -요.
성함이 어떻게 되세요?
seong-ha-mi eo-tteo-ke doe-se-yo
"May I have your name?" Front desks, clinic receptions and booking counters use it all day. 성함 is the honorific word for 이름 (name), and 되세요 carries the honorific -시-. Answer with your own name and a plain ending: OO예요 or OO이에요. Never use 성함 for your own name. The hotel words chapter (in Korean Words for Travel) has the other counter vocabulary.
몇 분이세요?
myeot bu-ni-se-yo
"How many people?" Staff ask this as you walk into a restaurant. 분 is the honorific counter for people. Answer with the plain counter 명: 두 명이요 (du myeong-i-yo, "two people"). Saying 두 분이요 about your own group is a classic slip, because you would be honoring yourselves. Ordering lines follow in the restaurant phrases chapter.
포장해 드릴까요?
po-jang-hae deu-ril-kka-yo
"Shall I wrap it for you?" or "Would you like it to go?" 드리다 is the humble form of 주다 (to give), used when the speaker does something for someone higher. Staff use it toward customers all day. You do not need to use 드리다 when you ask for things; for that, -주세요 is right. Answer with 네, 포장해 주세요 ("yes, to go, please") or 여기서 먹을게요 ("I'll eat here").
여기 앉으세요
yeo-gi an-jeu-se-yo
"Please sit here." The -(으)세요 ending turns a request into a respectful one, because it contains -시-. This is the line for offering your seat to an older person on a bus or subway. Compare 앉아요, which can sound like a flat instruction. If the person waves it away, 괜찮아요, just smile and leave it.
뭐 좀 여쭤봐도 될까요?
mwo jom yeo-jjwo-bwa-do doel-kka-yo
"May I ask you something?" 여쭤보다 is the humble verb for "to ask" someone you respect; the plain verb is 물어보다. It is the most polite way to open a question to an older stranger, a guard at a palace gate or a pharmacist.
식사하셨어요?
sik-sa-ha-syeo-sseo-yo
"Have you eaten?", a greeting rather than an invitation. 식사 is the formal word for a meal, and -셨- carries the honorific -시- in the past tense. Use it with an older host, a landlord or a colleague's parent. With a friend your own age, the casual version is 밥 먹었어?
