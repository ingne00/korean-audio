Book 1: Korean Phrases for Travel · Chapter 25

Guesthouses and Rentals

체크인 몇 시부터 돼요?
che-keu-in myeot si-bu-teo dwae-yo
"From what time can I check in?" 몇 시 is "what time", 부터 is "from" and 돼요 is "is it possible". Ask it in a message before you arrive, so you know whether to go straight to the room or drop your bags first. Small places often have one person doing the cleaning and the welcoming, so the answer can change from day to day. If the host replies with an hour, read it with the native number words: 세 시 is three o'clock, 네 시 is four.
짐 먼저 맡겨도 될까요?
jim meon-jeo mat-gyeo-do doel-kka-yo
"Can I leave my bags first?" 짐 is luggage, 먼저 is "first" or "before that", and 맡기다 is "to leave something in someone's care". The ending -어도 될까요 is a soft "would it be all right if". Use it when you arrive before check-in or want to drop your suitcase and go out. Some hosts have a corner of the entryway for bags; others will point you to a coin locker at a nearby station. Either way, ask before you leave anything outside the door.
현관 비밀번호가 뭐예요?
hyeon-gwan bi-mil-beon-ho-ga mwo-ye-yo
"What's the front door code?" 현관 is the front door and entryway of a house or flat, and 비밀번호 is a password or PIN. Most Korean flats and many guesthouses use a keypad lock instead of a key, so the door code is the one thing you cannot do without. Ask for it before the host leaves, and save it in your phone. If there is a second code for the building entrance, ask 1층 공동현관 비밀번호도 있어요? ("Is there a code for the shared door downstairs too?").
수건 더 받을 수 있을까요?
su-geon deo ba-deul su i-sseul-kka-yo
"Could I get more towels?" 수건 is towel, 더 is "more" and 받을 수 있을까요 is "could I receive". Swap the first word for anything else you need: 휴지 (toilet paper), 이불 (a blanket or duvet), 베개 (a pillow), 드라이기 (a hair dryer). For a stay of several nights, ask how towels and sheets are changed, since a home-share host may wash them only between guests. A short text with this line and the item's name works well.
분리수거는 어디에 하면 돼요?
bul-li-su-geo-neun eo-di-e ha-myeon dwae-yo
"Where do I put the recycling?" 분리수거 is sorted rubbish collection: plastics, cans, paper and food waste go in separate bins or bags. Each building has its own spot and its own days, and visitors get it wrong all the time, so this is a question hosts are glad to hear. Ask it on the first evening, and ask about food waste in particular with 음식물 쓰레기는요? ("And food waste?").
체크아웃은 몇 시까지예요?
che-keu-a-u-seun myeot si-kka-ji-ye-yo
"By what time do I check out?" 까지 is "until" or "by", the opposite of 부터 on card 1. Ask it on arrival rather than on the last morning. If you need to leave your bags after checkout, follow it with 체크아웃 후에 짐 좀 맡겨도 돼요? ("Can I leave my bags after checkout?"). Ask also what to do with the key card or the sheets: 열쇠는 어디에 두면 돼요? is "Where should I leave the key?".
무슨 일 있으면 어디로 연락드리면 돼요?
mu-seun il i-sseu-myeon eo-di-ro yeol-lak-deu-ri-myeon dwae-yo
"If anything comes up, where should I contact you?" 무슨 일 is "anything" in the sense of trouble, and 연락드리다 is the polite form of "to contact", used when you contact someone you are being polite to. The host will usually name an app or a phone number. Save it before the host leaves. If the host does not answer and you need help in English, VisitKorea lists the 1330 Korea Travel Hotline at +82-2-1330, with phone and chat help in several languages (VisitKorea).
