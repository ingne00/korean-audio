Subway Transfers and the Last Train

강남역 가려면 어디서 갈아타요?
gang-nam-nyeok ga-ryeo-myeon eo-di-seo ga-ra-ta-yo
"Where do I transfer to get to Gangnam Station?" 갈아타다 is the everyday verb for changing trains; the sign word is 환승 (hwan-seung). -려면 means "if I want to". Put any station name in front, followed by 역 (station). The subway words chapter (in Korean Words for Travel) has the other sign words you will see.
2호선 타는 곳이 어디예요?
i-ho-seon ta-neun go-si eo-di-ye-yo
"Where do I get Line 2?" 호선 is the word for a numbered line, and 타는 곳 ("the place where you board") is what the platform signs say. Lines also have colors on every sign, so once you know your line's color, follow it. If the line has a name rather than a number, say the name: 신분당선 타는 곳이 어디예요?
이거 잠실 방향 맞아요?
i-geo jam-sil bang-hyang ma-ja-yo
"Is this going toward Jamsil?" On most lines each platform serves one direction, and the signs name the next station and the direction, often with 방면 (bang-myeon, "bound for"). On Line 2, which is a loop, the signs say 내선 (inner circle) and 외선 (outer circle), so this question is worth asking. Point at the train or the platform when you say 이거.
몇 정거장 더 가야 돼요?
myeot jeong-geo-jang deo ga-ya dwae-yo
"How many more stops do I have to go?" Useful when a passenger tells you to get off "soon". The answer will be a native Korean number: 두 정거장 (two stops), 세 정거장 (three stops). 정거장 is the spoken word for a stop; 역 is the station.
여기서 내리면 돼요?
yeo-gi-seo nae-ri-myeon dwae-yo
"Do I get off here?" Ask it as the train pulls into a station you are unsure of. The announcement will also say 이번 역은 ... 역입니다 ("this station is ...") and 내리실 문은 오른쪽입니다 or 왼쪽입니다 ("the doors open on the right / left"). Listen for 오른쪽 and 왼쪽 to know which side to stand on.
막차 몇 시예요?
mak-cha myeot si-ye-yo
"What time is the last train?" 막차 is the last train or bus of the day, and 첫차 (cheot-cha) is the first. Last trains differ by line, station and direction, so check the Seoul Metro app or a map app for your own station. If you need a transfer to get home, the last train on the second line matters as much as the first.
카드가 안 찍혀요
ka-deu-ga an jji-kyeo-yo
"My card won't tap." Say it to the attendant at the gate or into the help phone on the gate. Common causes are a low balance, two cards in one wallet, or a missed tap at the last exit. If the balance is the problem, the T-money recharge chapter has the machine steps.
