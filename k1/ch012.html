Book 1: Korean Phrases for Travel · Chapter 12

Departure and Check-In

2터미널 어떻게 가요?
i-teo-mi-neol eo-tteo-ke ga-yo
"How do I get to Terminal 2?" Koreans say 1터미널 (il-teo-mi-neol) and 2터미널 (i-teo-mi-neol), or the fuller 제1여객터미널 and 제2여객터미널 you will see on signs. If you reach the wrong one, the airport runs a shuttle bus and the airport railroad between the two terminals (Incheon Airport terminal transfer page), so leave extra time.
대한항공 카운터가 어디예요?
dae-han-hang-gong ka-un-teo-ga eo-di-ye-yo
"Where is the Korean Air counter?" Swap in your airline: 아시아나항공 (a-si-a-na-hang-gong), 진에어 (ji-ne-eo), 제주항공 (je-ju-hang-gong). Counters are grouped by letter, so staff may simply answer with one, such as H 카운터. Asiana moved to Terminal 2 on January 14, 2026, and uses counters G to J there (Korea Times).
여권 여기 있어요
yeo-gwon yeo-gi i-sseo-yo
"Here is my passport." Hand it over open at the photo page, together with your booking number or e-ticket on your phone. The staff member will usually ask 어디까지 가세요? (eo-di-kka-ji ga-se-yo), "Where are you flying to?", to confirm the destination.
창가 자리로 주세요
chang-ga ja-ri-ro ju-se-yo
"A window seat, please." For an aisle seat, say 통로 쪽 자리로 주세요 (tong-no jjok ja-ri-ro ju-se-yo). To sit with a companion, say 같이 앉게 해 주세요 (ga-chi an-ge hae ju-se-yo), "Please let us sit together." If you checked in online and already have a seat, you can skip this line.
짐 하나 부칠게요
jim ha-na bu-chil-ge-yo
"I'll check one bag." 부치다 means to send off or check a bag. Use 두 개 (du gae, two) for a second bag. To ask about weight, say 무게 괜찮아요? (mu-ge gwaen-cha-na-yo), "Is the weight OK?". Allowances differ by airline and fare, so check your ticket before you pack. If you bought duty free online, the duty free pickup phrases cover the next stop.
보조 배터리는 기내에 가지고 타면 되죠?
bo-jo bae-teo-ri-neun gi-nae-e ga-ji-go ta-myeon doe-jyo
"I take the power bank on board with me, right?" 보조 배터리 is a power bank and 기내 is the cabin. Power banks are not allowed in checked bags. On Korean airlines departing Korea, they also may not go in the overhead bins, and their ports must be taped or the bank kept in a clear plastic bag (Korea JoongAng Daily). Under ICAO guidance that took effect on April 20, 2026, a passenger may carry no more than two power banks in the cabin, and charging or using them during the flight is banned (Korea Times). Check your airline's page for its exact limits.
몇 번 게이트예요?
myeot beon ge-i-teu-ye-yo
"Which gate number is it?" The answer comes in Sino-Korean numbers: 이십오 번 (i-si-bo beon) is gate 25. Gates at Incheon can be a long walk from security, so check the number as soon as you have your boarding pass and look for it on the signs before you stop to shop. Before the gate comes security and the passport check, and the immigration phrases help if an officer asks you something.
