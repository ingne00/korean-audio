Bargaining at a Market

Voice:
이거 얼마예요?
i-geo eol-ma-ye-yo
"How much is this?" 이거 is "this thing", 얼마 is "how much" and 예요 is "it is". Point at the item or pick it up as you say it, so there is no doubt which one you mean. The answer comes back in Sino-Korean numbers with 원 (won): 만 원 is 10,000 won and 오천 원 is 5,000 won. If the number goes past you, hold out your phone calculator and the vendor will usually type it. The number words in my number words chapter (in Korean Words for Travel) help with prices.
두 개 사면 얼마예요?
du gae sa-myeon eol-ma-ye-yo
"How much if I buy two?" This is the most natural bargaining move at a market stall. The vendor sells more, you pay less per piece, and nobody has to argue. 개 is the general counter for things, and with a counter Korean uses native numbers: 한 개, 두 개, 세 개 (one, two, three pieces). Swap 두 for 세 if you want three.
좀 깎아 주세요
jom kka-kka ju-se-yo
"Please take a little off." 깎다 means to cut or trim, and 깎아 주다 is the everyday verb for giving a discount. 좀 ("a little") makes it soft. Say it once, with a smile. If the vendor says 안 돼요 (an dwae-yo, "I can't") or 이게 제일 싼 거예요 ("this is already the lowest"), take that as the answer and decide whether to buy.
카드 돼요?
ka-deu dwae-yo
"Do you take card?" Literally "Does a card work?" Ask before you agree on a price. Some stalls prefer cash or a bank transfer, and it is better to know before you start counting notes. If you need cash, the lines in my ATM phrases chapter cover foreign cards at Korean machines.
하나만 더 주시면 안 돼요?
ha-na-man deo ju-si-myeon an dwae-yo
"Could you throw in one more?" Word for word: "Would it not work if you gave me just one more?" The negative question makes it a gentle request, the way a regular customer might ask. It fits small things sold by count, like socks, fruit or rice cakes. Market vendors sometimes add a small extra on their own, and Korean has a word for it: 덤 (deom), a little something given on top of what you paid for.
다른 데 좀 보고 올게요
da-reun de jom bo-go ol-ge-yo
"I'll look around a bit and come back." 데 means "place", so the line is literally "I'll look at another place and come." It is the polite way to leave when the price does not work for you. Walking away in silence after a long chat feels abrupt; this line closes the conversation on good terms, and sometimes the vendor answers with a lower price.
이걸로 주세요
i-geol-lo ju-se-yo
"I'll take this one." 이걸로 is the spoken form of 이것으로, "with this thing". Point to the exact piece, especially with clothes or fruit, where each item is a little different. To ask for a bag, add 봉투 주세요 (bong-tu ju-se-yo).
