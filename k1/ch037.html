Exchanges and Refunds

Voice:
환불돼요?
hwan-bul-dwae-yo
"Can I get a refund?" 환불 is a refund on something you bought, and 환불되다 is "to be refunded", so this asks whether a refund is possible at all. It is the safest first line, because return rules vary by shop. Many receipts have the shop's exchange and refund period printed near the bottom; look for 교환 and 환불 next to a number of days.
교환하고 싶어요
gyo-hwan-ha-go si-peo-yo
"I'd like to exchange it." 교환 is the word for swapping goods, and -고 싶어요 is "I want to". Use it when you want a different size, color or item, not your money back. If the clerk asks 어떤 걸로요? ("for which one?"), point to the item you want.
영수증 여기 있어요
yeong-su-jeung yeo-gi i-sseo-yo
"Here is the receipt." Put it on the counter with the item. The receipt shows the date, the price and the payment method, which is everything the clerk needs to process a return. If you paid by card and lost the receipt, offer the same card; some shops can look up the sale that way, and some cannot.
사이즈가 안 맞아요
sa-i-jeu-ga an ma-ja-yo
"The size doesn't fit." 맞다 means "to fit" or "to be right", and 안 makes it negative. To be more exact, say 너무 커요 ("too big") or 너무 작아요 ("too small"). My clothing store words chapter (in Korean Words for Travel) has the size and fitting words, and for sleeves or hems you would rather shorten, see my tailor shop phrases chapter.
한 사이즈 큰 걸로 바꿔 주세요
han sa-i-jeu keun geol-lo ba-kkwo ju-se-yo
"Please swap it for one size bigger." 바꾸다 means "to change" or "to swap", and in shops 바꿔 주세요 is as common as 교환해 주세요. For a smaller size, use 작은 (ja-geun) in place of 큰. For a color, say 다른 색으로 바꿔 주세요 ("swap it for another color").
이거 불량이에요
i-geo bul-lyang-i-e-yo
"This one is faulty." 불량 covers anything defective: a seam that is coming apart, a zip that sticks, a stain that was there from the start. Point to the spot and add 여기요 ("here"). A fault is the strongest reason for an exchange or refund, so say it first when it applies.
카드 결제 취소해 주세요
ka-deu gyeol-je chwi-so-hae ju-se-yo
"Please cancel the card payment." In Korea a card refund is usually done as a cancellation of the original payment, so this line tells the clerk how you want the money back. Bring the card you paid with. The cancellation shows on your statement once the card company processes it, which can take some days with a foreign card.
