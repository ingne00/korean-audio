Book 1: Korean Phrases for Travel · Chapter 34

The Mart

봉투 얼마예요?
bong-tu eol-ma-ye-yo
"How much is a bag?" Say it before the last item is scanned. Follow with 큰 걸로 하나 주세요 (keun geol-lo ha-na ju-se-yo, "one big one, please") or 작은 걸로 하나 주세요 (ja-geun geol-lo, "one small one"). If you brought your own, say 장바구니 가져왔어요 (jang-ba-gu-ni ga-jyeo-wa-sseo-yo, "I brought my own bag") or 봉투는 괜찮아요 (bong-tu-neun gwaen-cha-na-yo, "I don't need a bag").
영수증 주세요
yeong-su-jeung ju-se-yo
"A receipt, please." 영수증 is the slip that shows you paid; the National Institute of Korean Language dictionary defines it as a document showing that money or goods were received. If the cashier asks 영수증 드릴까요?, a plain 네, 주세요 works. If you do not want it, say 괜찮아요.
포인트 적립돼요?
po-in-teu jeong-nip-dwae-yo
"Can I collect points?" 적립 means piling points up, and 돼요 means "is it possible". If the cashier asks 멤버십 있으세요? (mem-beo-sip i-sseu-se-yo, "do you have a membership?"), you can answer 없어요 (eop-seo-yo, "I don't have one"). To decline, say 적립은 괜찮아요 (jeong-ni-beun gwaen-cha-na-yo, "no points, thanks").
카드로 일시불이요
ka-deu-ro il-si-bu-ri-yo
"By card, in one payment." 일시불 (il-si-bul) is the word for paying the whole amount at once; the dictionary defines it as paying or settling an amount all at once. The other choice you may hear is 할부 (hal-bu, installments), so 일시불이요 tells the cashier you want the normal full payment. If you would rather pay by phone, ask 모바일 결제돼요? (mo-ba-il gyeol-je-dwae-yo, "does mobile payment work?").
라면 어디 있어요?
ra-myeon eo-di i-sseo-yo
"Where is the ramyeon?" Swap in any product: 김 (gim, seasoned laver), 과자 (gwa-ja, snacks), 우유 (u-yu, milk). To find the checkout, ask 계산대 어디예요? (gye-san-dae eo-di-ye-yo). A typical answer is 저쪽이에요 (jeo-jjo-gi-e-yo, "it's over there"), usually with a pointing hand.
이거 좀 달아 주세요
i-geo jom da-ra ju-se-yo
"Could you weigh this for me, please?" The verb 달다 has a dictionary entry meaning to find a weight with a scale, and 저울 (jeo-ul) is the scale. A follow-up is 몇 킬로예요? (myeot kil-lo-ye-yo, "how many kilos is it?"). Use it for loose fruit or vegetables when you cannot tell how the price is worked out.
몇 시에 문 닫아요?
myeot si-e mun da-da-yo
"What time do you close?" A close cousin is 몇 시까지 해요? (myeot si-kka-ji hae-yo, "until what time are you open?"). For a day off, ask 오늘 문 열어요? (o-neul mun yeo-reo-yo, "are you open today?"). Hours and closed days depend on the branch.
