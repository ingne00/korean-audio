Book 1: Korean Phrases for Travel · Chapter 56

Calling 112 and 119

도와주세요!
do-wa-ju-se-yo
"Help me!" or "Please help!" Shout it in the street to get people around you to act, and say it as your first words on the phone. It works for both 112 and 119, and it tells the dispatcher at once that this is not a routine call.
구급차 불러 주세요
gu-geup-cha bul-leo ju-se-yo
"Please call an ambulance." 구급차 is an ambulance. Say this to a shop clerk, a taxi driver or a passerby if you cannot make the call yourself, or say it on the phone to a 119 dispatcher to make your need plain. Ambulance crews are part of the fire service, so 119 is the number for both.
불이 났어요
bu-ri na-sseo-yo
"There's a fire." 불 is fire and 나다 is "to break out". After this line, give the place (card 6) and say whether anyone is still inside: 안에 사람 있어요 (a-ne sa-ram i-sseo-yo), "There are people inside." Then get out of the building and stay out.
친구가 많이 다쳤어요
chin-gu-ga ma-ni da-chyeo-sseo-yo
"My friend is badly hurt." Replace 친구 with 사람 (sa-ram, a person) for a stranger. If the person is not breathing, say 숨을 안 쉬어요 (su-meul an swi-eo-yo), "They are not breathing." For symptoms and body parts, the pharmacy phrases have more lines.
누가 따라와요
nu-ga tta-ra-wa-yo
"Someone is following me." This is a 112 line. Stay in a lit place with people, such as a convenience store, while you call, and tell the dispatcher where you are. If speaking out loud is unsafe, you can text 112 instead; the questions at the end explain how.
여기는 시청 앞이에요
yeo-gi-neun si-cheong a-pi-e-yo
"I'm in front of City Hall." Swap 시청 for any landmark you can see: a hotel name, a station exit (3번 출구, sam-beon chul-gu, Exit 3) or a shop sign. A landmark you can name helps the crew find you on the street. The emergency words (in Korean Words for Travel) include more place words.
영어 통역 부탁드려요
yeong-eo tong-yeok bu-tak-deu-ryeo-yo
"English interpretation, please." Police interpretation on 112 covers English and Chinese, and since March 2024 it has run around the clock instead of only on weekdays. Use it as your second line, right after the first thing you shout, and stay on the call while the interpreter joins.
