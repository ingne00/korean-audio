The Pharmacy

머리가 아파요
meo-ri-ga a-pa-yo
"I have a headache" (literally "my head hurts"). This is the pattern for every body part: body word + 가/이 + 아파요. 배가 아파요 (bae-ga a-pa-yo) is stomach, 목이 아파요 (mo-gi a-pa-yo) is throat, 이가 아파요 (i-ga a-pa-yo) is tooth. Pointing and saying 여기가 아파요 (yeo-gi-ga a-pa-yo, "it hurts here") works too.
배가 아파요
bae-ga a-pa-yo
"My stomach hurts." For an upset stomach after eating, 배탈이 났어요 (bae-ta-ri na-sseo-yo) is the common phrase. For diarrhea, say 설사를 해요 (seol-sa-reul hae-yo). VisitKorea opens its pharmacy guide with traveler's diarrhea and an upset stomach after new food, so this is a line many visitors end up needing.
감기약 있어요?
gam-gi-yak i-sseo-yo
"Do you have cold medicine?" To describe yourself, say 감기에 걸렸어요 (gam-gi-e geol-lyeo-sseo-yo, "I caught a cold"). Swap the first word for 소화제 (so-hwa-je, digestive aid) or 진통제 (jin-tong-je, painkiller) to ask for those.
처방전 없어도 살 수 있어요?
cheo-bang-jeon eop-seo-do sal su i-sseo-yo
"Can I buy it without a prescription?" 처방전 (cheo-bang-jeon) is the prescription slip from a doctor. Point at a box and say 이거요 (i-geo-yo, "this one").
하루에 몇 번 먹어요?
ha-ru-e myeot beon meo-geo-yo
"How many times a day do I take it?" A typical answer is 하루에 세 번이요 (ha-ru-e se beo-ni-yo, "three times a day"), sometimes with 식후 30분 (sik-hu sam-sip-bun, "30 minutes after a meal").
멀미약 있어요?
meol-mi-yak i-sseo-yo
"Do you have motion sickness medicine?" For a hangover, ask 숙취해소제 있어요? (suk-chwi-hae-so-je i-sseo-yo), where 숙취 means hangover and 해소제 means a relief product.
저는 페니실린 알레르기가 있어요
jeo-neun pe-ni-sil-lin al-le-reu-gi-ga i-sseo-yo
"I am allergic to penicillin." Replace 페니실린 with the item that affects you: 아스피린 (a-seu-pi-rin) for aspirin, 땅콩 (ttang-kong) for peanuts. VisitKorea says to tell the pharmacist your allergies, so say this before you accept any box.
