Korean BBQ Restaurants

두 명 자리 있어요?
du myeong ja-ri i-sseo-yo
"Do you have a table for two?" Swap 두 (du, two) for 한 (han, one), 세 (se, three) or 네 (ne, four) in front of 명. Staff often ask you first: 몇 분이세요? (myeot bu-ni-se-yo, "how many people?"), and 두 명이에요 (du myeong-i-e-yo, "it is two of us") is the answer.
삼겹살 2인분 주세요
sam-gyeop-sal i-in-bun ju-se-yo
"Two servings of pork belly, please." Replace 삼겹살 with the cut on the menu and 2 with 3 (삼인분, sa-min-bun) for a bigger group. You can also just point at the menu line and say 이거 2인분 주세요 (i-geo i-in-bun ju-se-yo, "two servings of this").
불판 갈아 주세요
bul-pan ga-ra ju-se-yo
"Please change the grill plate." SeoulStart says to ask when the plate is heavily charred or before switching from plain to marinated meat, because marinade sugar burns fast on a used plate. Gopchang Story, a US restaurant, says staff will usually do it without being asked.
반찬 더 주세요
ban-chan deo ju-se-yo
"More side dishes, please." For one dish, name it: 김치 더 주세요 (gim-chi deo ju-se-yo, "more kimchi, please"). Lettuce and shredded scallion are the ones that run out fastest, according to a July 2026 essay on how BBQ works.
물은 셀프예요
mu-reun sel-peu-ye-yo
"Water is self-service." This is something you will hear from staff, and the useful reply is 어디 있어요? (eo-di i-sseo-yo, "where is it?"). Look for a dispenser and a stack of cups, pour your own, and bring it back to the table.
안 맵게 해 주세요
an maep-ge hae ju-se-yo
"Please make it not spicy." KoreanClass101 teaches this exact phrase for dishes you would rather have mild, and 이거 매워요? (i-geo mae-wo-yo, "is this spicy?") for checking first. For a middle path, 덜 맵게 해 주세요 (deol maep-ge hae ju-se-yo) means "less spicy". Plain grilled meat is not usually the problem; heat tends to come from marinades, dipping sauces and any stew you add, so ask about those.
계산해 주세요
gye-san-hae ju-se-yo
"Please give me the bill." Daebak lists this and the shorter 계산이요 (gye-sa-ni-yo) as the ways to ask. In most Korean restaurants you walk to the front counter to pay, not at the table. A friendlier version at the register is 계산할게요 (gye-san-hal-ge-yo, "I will pay").
